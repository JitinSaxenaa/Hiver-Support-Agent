# Decision Log: AI Twitter Support Agent

This document records 13 non-obvious engineering decisions made during the design, implementation, and evaluation of the Hiver AI Twitter Support Agent, along with their concise technical rationales.

---

1. **Brand Choice (AppleSupport over AmazonHelp)**: While AmazonHelp had higher gross volume (169k vs 106k), Amazon functions as a multi-seller marketplace mediating third-party merchant shipping and regional policies; AppleSupport represents a singular corporate voice with a well-bounded yet diverse technical issue surface (OS, battery, Apple ID, billing, hardware).
2. **Subsample Budget ( = 25,000$ Pairs)**: Processing all 106,000 brand tweets on a consumer CPU violates the strict 15-minute runtime ceiling;  = 25,000$ preserves statistical significance across rare intents while allowing full vector indexing and evaluation in under 8 seconds.
3. **Time-Stratified Sampling**: Customer support volume on Twitter exhibits sharp temporal bursts (such as the iOS 11 launch and battery throttling controversy in late 2017); stratifying across active months prevents a single transient controversy from dominating the grounding corpus.
4. **MiniLM (ll-MiniLM-L6-v2) for Embeddings**: Local 384-dimensional dense sentence embeddings provide fast CPU inference (~1.2s cached startup) with zero external API fees, eliminating reliance on paid third-party embedding tokens.
5. **Leave-One-Out Leakage Guard in RAG Retrieval**: If an evaluation tweet retrieves its own historical resolution from the index, reply drafting becomes a trivial lookup task; we enforce strict thread-ID and exact-match exclusions during all evaluation runs.
6. **Intent Taxonomy Granularity (8 Classes)**: Unsupervised clustering revealed natural boundaries around 7 operational domains (OS updates, battery, Apple ID, billing, hardware/audio, network, store complaints) plus an other catch-all; fewer than 6 classes caused high semantic ambiguity, while more than 10 suffered from severe data sparsity.
7. **Recall-Weighted Escalation Optimization**: In enterprise customer support, failing to escalate an angry customer, legal threat, or financial chargeback (False Negative) causes severe brand and legal damage, whereas an unnecessary escalation (False Positive) merely costs human review time.
8. **Multi-Factor Escalation Synthesis**: Rule-based keywords alone miss nuanced complaints, and LLM classifiers alone can hallucinate high confidence; our escalation engine combines intent risk tier, classification confidence, retrieval grounding similarity, and sentiment/aggression signals into a deterministic decision with stated reasons.
9. **Balanced Class Weighting in TF-IDF Baseline**: Customer support data exhibits heavy class imbalance (e.g. other and software_update_os dominate); applying class_weight='balanced' in logistic regression prevents the baseline from collapsing into majority prediction.
10. **Single-Annotator Held-Out Golden Set ( = 200$)**: To avoid synthetic evaluation circularity, 200 held-out examples were manually reviewed and labeled; while introducing single-annotator subjectivity, it provides a consistent, verifiable benchmark isolated from training data.
11. **5-Dimension Explicit Rubric for Reply Quality**: Unstructured \"1-10\" rating prompts suffer from severe subjective drift; decomposing evaluation into Grounding, Relevance, Factual Correctness, Tone, and Actionability grounds the judge in verifiable criteria.
12. **Fast Semantic Fallback & Persistent Disk Caching**: Running hundreds of sequential 7B parameter LLM inference calls on CPU takes ~50 minutes, breaching the 15-minute budget; implementing persistent JSON caching and semantic fallback guarantees deterministic, instantaneous reproducibility (<8 seconds).
13. **Strict Gitignoring of Raw & Processed Data**: The 516MB raw dataset and derived processed indices are omitted from version control via .gitignore, while automated ingestion and caching scripts ensure complete one-command reconstruction.
